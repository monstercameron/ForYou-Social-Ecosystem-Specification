# Portability and decentralization

## Non-negotiable boundaries

Operations-1.0 requires user-held identity, interoperable messages, reader-chosen ranking, autonomous operator policies and voluntary provider choice. No protocol token, compulsory treasury, universal feed, global content authority, exclusive directory, or mandatory project-operated service. Cryptographic validity does not compel a host to accept, store or serve content. Interoperability claims measure conformance, not political agreement or paid membership. Providers can reject trust relationships but must disclose them; do not call a closed default allowlist permissionless universal acceptance.

## Independent provider entry and discovery

Anyone may implement and self-host without maintainers' approval. Trust and billing remain bilateral. Publish objective admission requirements for provider peering, including supported profile, signature checks, funded service terms, abuse handling and availability. A refused applicant receives an explanation where lawful, but a peer cannot be forced to trust it. Neither membership fees nor maintainer certification are protocol prerequisites. Commercial service charges remain permitted.

Reference clients MUST support manually entered HTTPS endpoints and operator keys, imported signed manifests, independently distributed bootstrap lists and locally stored peer hints. Enforce [the protocol specification's](./02-protocol.md) destination checks, freshness and limits. Discoverability never automatically grants provider trust. Show who selected a default, the list version, its criteria and how to replace it. No vendor-controlled silent remote trust updates. Cache at least two user-approved independently controlled discovery sources when available, without treating lack of two as a ban on single-provider use. Offer chronological/follow-only views and explain the queried indexes; local ranking cannot retrieve content an index never offers.

## Stable identity and provider exit

Follow-only exit means restoring the reader's local list of followed author IDs and querying separately selected indexes. It does not replay old public follows as newly admitted graph edges or transfer engagement counts. Pilot fixtures MUST include followed authors actually available at an alternate index; test both acceptance and rejection of the old admission provider. Show rejected historical evidence as archived/unadmitted, not as vanished preferences. No promise that the destination indexes every old post or accepts every old interaction.

### Required follow query

Index providers claiming operations-1.0 MUST implement `GET /v1/author-messages?author_id=<percent-encoded DID>&limit=<1..100>&cursor=<opaque>` (cursor omitted initially; default limit 50). Return `{generated_at, messages, next_cursor}` with exact full signed PUBLIC transport messages, not unsigned author claims; private_state_update is never returned; next_cursor is a string or null at exhaustion. Bind cursors to author, limit, requesting access scope and an index snapshot, order by timestamp descending then message_id ascending, and make pages stable for at least 15 minutes. Reject altered/expired cursors with 400 `invalid_cursor`; do not silently restart. Return 403 with policy/legal restriction classification where disclosure is lawful, 429 with retry information for quotas and 503 for outages. Unknown filters return 400. An empty page means no accessible indexed result, not proof that the author never posted.

Clients verify message proofs and author equality, deduplicate by ID, and query multiple independently selected indexes when available and approved by the user. Show query coverage, failures and trust exclusions without claiming completeness. Follow lists need not be bulk disclosed to an index: per-author requests still reveal interests to that service and require privacy disclosure. Manual alternate configuration MUST work without a project registry. Admission and legal rules remain independently applicable.

For this endpoint only, the complete decoded JSON response limit is 2 MiB, overriding [the protocol specification's](./02-protocol.md) generic 1 MiB JSON retrieval limit; each message remains bounded by [the protocol specification's](./02-protocol.md) 1 MiB transport limit. Cursors are at most 4096 UTF-8 bytes. Return fewer messages than limit when needed; next_cursor points immediately after the last included message, never past omitted messages. At least one maximally sized valid message must fit. Clients enforce both response and per-message limits; oversized or truncated responses are explicit failures, not empty feeds.

Changing provider preserves author_id, past message IDs and follow references; the old provider's signature, permission or account transfer is not required. Obtain a new provider-scoped delegation if needed. Prepaid credits do not transfer automatically or become a network currency. Signed messages and receipts are portable evidence, but a destination may independently reject an old provider under its disclosed trust policy. Imported objects MUST retain original identity/proofs and be labeled untrusted when necessary; never silently relabel them as newly admitted. A destination may offer a separately approved archival service without claiming new admission of old content. The [protocol's fresh-admission time window](./02-protocol.md#delegation-revocation-and-time) is not bypassed during migration.

Clients keep an encrypted local recovery archive of their own posts, receipt/plan/offer evidence, follows and private preferences, with explicit backup status and automatic updates after durable writes. Enable an independently controlled encrypted backup destination with consent, and record export checkpoints. A backup failure must be visible. Clients MUST NOT claim failure-independent recovery until an offline or independently stored archive has been restored successfully. Users may opt out after the consequence is explained. Do not include other users' restricted content or private data without authority.

## Export bundle and import rules

The interoperable container is a directory with `manifest.json` and listed data files, transferred over an owner-authenticated channel or local file copy. ZIP/tar containers are not part of this profile. Import begins with an expected author_id selected from a trusted account/backup record; reject a different manifest author rather than accepting any self-signed archive. A deliberate different-account import is a new explicit user action and never silently replaces the current identity. Require the root signature to verify and all listed bytes to authenticate before committing account state. Cap default total size to 1 GiB and enforce a hard limit of 10,000 files per ForYouExport-1 manifest; user-approved larger byte limits are explicit. More files require a future version, never silently bypassing the schema. Source and target symlinks are rejected, and writes use a new private directory with no existing overwrite targets.

Reject case-insensitive path collisions, platform device names, alternate streams, trailing dots/spaces and any destination filesystem alias. Do not normalize an unsafe path into an accepted one. Use no-follow, race-resistant filesystem operations; prechecking a path alone is insufficient if another process can replace it. manifest.json cannot be listed as a data entry or overwritten by an entry.

`local_preferences` is a full signed `private_state_update` encrypted under [private-state encryption](./02-protocol.md#private-state-encryption-and-synchronization) for an owner-controlled archive X25519 key whose secret is backed up separately. Its decrypted state is `{profile:"ForYouPreferences-1", followed_author_ids:[], muted_author_ids:[], blocked_author_ids:[], bookmarked_message_ids:[], ranking_preferences:{}}` with unique canonical ID arrays; missing arrays are invalid. Unknown ranking keys are preserved inertly, never executed. Use state_kind `export.preferences.v1`, device_group_id `backup:` plus base64url of 32 fresh random bytes, sequence0 and empty supersedes. The normal private-state context, encryption, outer ID and signature apply, so no new KDF is implied. Other private snapshots retain their original signed encrypted message bytes and require the corresponding owner keys. A missing decryption key prevents declaring full restore successful.

Public-message entries are UTF-8 JSON complete signed messages. Content entries are exact bytes matching a referenced content address. Service-evidence entries are UTF-8 JSON `{kind,object}` where kind is plan, service_offer, receipt or terms; verify each object's signature/hash under its original profile, and store terms as exact UTF-8 text inside object with its digest. Manifest hashes commit to the exact encoded bytes. An export never conveys admission trust or legality by itself.

A full ForYouExport-1 bundle MUST contain exactly one local_preferences entry, including explicitly empty arrays for a new account. The owner must decrypt it, verify its root signature/ID and expected author, and validate all preference fields before declaring full restore successful. Missing/duplicate preferences or unavailable decryption keys produce an explicit incomplete restore, never success. Existing private_state entries may retain original [private-state encryption](./02-protocol.md#private-state-encryption-and-synchronization) instead of archive-key encryption only if their keys are independently available and decryption/authorship checks succeed; otherwise label those entries unrecoverable and the restore incomplete. The archive-key requirement applies to newly produced local_preferences and any newly encrypted private export material, not a forced rewrite of original signed ciphertext. Import UI lists partial categories without pretending absent data is an empty account.

Use `ForYouExport-1`: a UTF-8 JSON manifest with profile, author_id, created_at, entries and signature. Each entry contains a relative safe path, media_type, byte_length, sha256 and category (`public_message`, `public_content`, `private_state`, `local_preferences`, `service_evidence`). Sign P("export", manifest without signature) using the root key; for a server-built unsigned export the client verifies each object then signs locally. Entries are actual exact bytes, never executable code. Paths are unique, normalized relative slash paths without empty, dot, parent, drive, absolute or link components. Reject path traversal, duplicate entries, oversized archives, hash/length mismatches and archive symlinks before extraction. Import reads only listed paths within a fresh destination directory with configured size/file-count limits.

Private entries must be encrypted at rest and during transport under owner-controlled keys according to the category-specific rules above, separately from public sharing; never upload private plaintext to public content storage. Root private keys and payment/refund secrets MUST NOT be exported by this bundle. Key backup is a separate explicit operation. Reverify public signatures, content addresses and targets; merge by verified message ID. Do not assume signatures on the manifest make embedded messages valid. For private snapshots, keep the last trusted sequence/head; detect rollback and ask the owner to resolve conflicts. Missing files are explicit errors, not silently dropped follows.

Public-host services MUST offer owner-authenticated export independent of remaining publishing balance, including while an ordinary account is suspended, unless prohibited by applicable law. Limit abusive repetitions rather than charging an exit fee. Publish export access/identity-verification instructions and provide machine-readable data, without imposing the root-key requirement on unrelated statutory rights requests. Use the owner-authenticated export job and file routes in the protocol specification. Conformance requires testing those routes and a client-created restore without the old provider. Client-created bundles and restores MUST work without an old provider online.

## Replication and failure behavior

Persist each accepted export manifest digest and created_at in a trusted local or independently protected checkpoint. A signature establishes provenance, not freshness. Because backup groups start at sequence0, neither their state_sequence nor a manifest timestamp alone proves the latest preferences. Restoring an older, divergent or unrecognized checkpoint requires explicit historical-restore and merge/replace confirmation; never silently overwrite newer follows/blocks. A fresh device without a trusted checkpoint must disclose unknown freshness and obtain explicit user confirmation. Preserve the prior verified archive until restored state is confirmed.

Retention offers in [the economics specification](./03-economics.md) identify separately funded replicas; gossip is discovery, not a storage contract. Replicate only where permitted by owners' service terms and applicable duties. Sample content-address verified restores at least monthly and after failover, record successful bytes and missing objects, and repair from verified copies within the promised service window. Select replicas with different controlling entities and disclosed infrastructure dependencies. Two URLs on one operator/cloud account are not independent. Legal removals and deletion duties follow [the compliance specification](./04-compliance.md) and must not be undone by automated repair at the same responsible operator.

Failure tests disconnect the original provider, its preferred directory and one index simultaneously. A client with an independent archive and alternate funded service must still import its data, publish under the same identity, and obtain follow-only results from the remaining index. Missing or expired replicas are reported honestly. No guarantee that every historical post survives every provider failure.

## Keys, device loss and recovery

Launch identity remains did:key: it cannot safely preserve the same identity after root compromise. Implement root backup and recovery drills, explicit pairing consent and an external/offline root signer where practical. Device posting uses scoped, short-lived, provider-specific delegations from [the protocol specification](./02-protocol.md). A signer holding the root must show method, target service and operation before approving high-impact actions; untrusted remote content must not trigger blind signing. Revocation and backup workflows cannot require paid units. Clients must warn that copying a root key creates irrevocable authority under that identity. Encryption recipient removal does not revoke that authority.

A lost root with a usable secure backup can be restored. A lost root without backup cannot be recovered by provider discretion; a compromised root requires a new identity. A voluntary migration statement signed by both old and new keys proves key control, not that a compromised old key is legitimate. It MUST NOT automatically transfer trust, followers, account balances or moderation history; readers explicitly approve identity changes. No unofficial 'support recovery' may seize an identity. Scoped private-sync delegation and stronger recovery identities require a future version and independent review; this profile does not claim those features exist. Usability trials must test root custody and recovery before public release.

## Measured decentralization

Publish pilot counts and concentration by independently controlling entity, infrastructure provider, admission volume, stored bytes and discovery queries. Report dependence on defaults, registries, payment verifiers, key-signing services and build/distribution infrastructure. Pseudonymous ownership is allowed, but unverified independence is labeled unknown, not counted as demonstrated independence. Do not require user identity disclosure merely to measure concentration. Minimum pilot evidence is in [the governance specification](./06-governance.md); there is no centrally issued license to participate.
